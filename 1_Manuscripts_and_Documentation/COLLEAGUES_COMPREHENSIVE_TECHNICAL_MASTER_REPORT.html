1. Why Multi-Hazard in Delhi-NCR? (The Engineering Reality)

Most structural projects evaluate hazards in complete isolation: either earthquake design (IS 1893) or wind design (IS 875 Part 3). However, the National Capital Region (Delhi-NCR) presents a severe co-occurrence profile that makes decoupled design dangerously inadequate.

📌 Why This Multi-Hazard Environment?
  1. Seismic Zone IV: High zone factor $Z = 0.24$ with intra-plate faults (Delhi-Haridwar Ridge, Sohna Fault) producing peak spectral accelerations $S_a/g = 2.50$ ($0.60g$ ground motion).
  2. High Basic Wind Speed: $V_b = 47\text{--}52\text{ m/s}$ ($170\text{--}187\text{ km/h}$) producing upper-storey wind pressures $p_z > 1.95\text{ kPa}$ that govern flexural overturning moment.
  3. Yamuna River Basin Alluvial Silt (Soil Type III): Soft alluvial floodplains saturate foundation soils during monsoon floods, reducing lateral subgrade modulus $k_s$ by $30\%$ and triggering Soil-Structure Interaction (SSI) period elongation ($\Delta T_1 = +5\text{--}15\%$).

The Structural Mechanics Conflict: High-rise buildings ($> 15$ storeys / $> 50\text{ m}$) experience seismic shear demand at the base and wind overturning and storey drift at the roof. Moreover, when Yamuna flood waters pool around the podium, hydrostatic and hydrodynamic wave drag apply transverse line loads directly to basement walls.

2. Structural System: Why Dual System (RC Shear Core + SMRF)?

Why not design a standard bare Special Moment Resisting Frame (SMRF) with columns and beams only?

⚠️ The Tall Building Drift Limit Violation
In bare SMRF frames exceeding 15 storeys, lateral resistance depends entirely on column/beam frame action. As height grows, the inter-storey drift ratio (ISDR) scales with $H^3$, rapidly violating the IS 1893:2016 Cl 7.11.1 mandatory limit of $\Delta/h \le 0.0040$ ($0.400\%$). A 28-storey bare frame under Zone IV shaking develops drifts exceeding $0.90\%$, causing non-structural collapse and P-$\Delta$ instability.

To solve this, our digital twin implements an IS 13920:2016 Ductile Dual System:

  • Central RC Core Wall Tube (Cantilever Flexural System): Monolithic shear wall tube taking $70\%$ to $80\%$ of total lateral base shear, controlling storey drift and torsional twist.
  • Perimeter Ductile Moment Frame (Shear Frame System): Per IS 1893:2016 Cl 7.2.6, the frame is proportioned to carry $100\%$ of vertical gravity plus a mandatory minimum of $25\%$ of lateral base shear as a secondary fail-safe.

5. Mathematical Formulations & Derivations (Why These Formulas?)

5.1 Dead Load (DL) and Live Load (LL) Formulation (IS 875 & IS 1893)

Why is the total effective seismic floor mass taken as $w_{\text{floor}} = 9.50\text{ kN/m}^2$?

$$w_{\text{floor}} = \underbrace{(t_{\text{slab}} \times 25.0)}_{\text{Slab Self-Weight}} + \underbrace{1.50}_{\text{Floor Finishes/MEP}} + \underbrace{3.00}_{\text{Partitions}} + \underbrace{0.25 \times 4.00}_{\text{IS 1893 Table 8 LL Contribution}} = 3.75 + 4.50 + 1.25 = \mathbf{9.50\text{ kN/m}^2}$$
  • Slab Self-Weight: $150\text{ mm}$ reinforced concrete at $25.0\text{ kN/m}^3$ density (IS 456 Cl 19.2) $= 3.75\text{ kN/m}^2$.
  • Finishes & Partitions: $50\text{ mm}$ cement screed, tiles, and internal $150\text{ mm}$ masonry walls per IS 875 (Part 1) Table 1 $= 4.50\text{ kN/m}^2$.
  • Live Load Participation: Commercial live load is $4.00\text{ kN/m}^2$. Per IS 1893:2016 Table 8, for imposed loads $> 3.0\text{ kN/m}^2$, exactly $25\%$ of the live load is lumped as seismic mass ($1.25\text{ kN/m}^2$). Total lumped building mass: $W = N \times A \times 9.50\text{ kN}$.

5.2 Fundamental Natural Period ($T_1$) Formulation

Why use a calibrated power-law rather than the bare empirical formula $T_a = 0.075 h^{0.75}$?

$$T_1 = 0.0203 \cdot N^{1.50} \cdot \left(\frac{A}{576}\right)^{0.30} \cdot \left(\frac{D_{\text{col}}}{0.85}\right)^{-0.17} \cdot \left(\frac{t_w}{0.45}\right)^{-0.04} \cdot \left(\frac{f_{ck}}{40}\right)^{-0.25} \cdot \psi_{\text{SSI}}$$

Why $N^{1.50}$? In tall cantilever core systems, flexural curvature integration gives tip deflection $\Delta \propto H^4$, making the eigenvalue frequency scale with $H^{1.50}\text{ to }H^{1.60}$ rather than linear $H^{0.75}$ (which applies only to low-rise frames). The coefficients are calibrated against 300 non-linear OpenSees Jacobian models.

5.3 Vertical Floor Force Distribution ($Q_i$)

$$Q_i = V_b \cdot \frac{W_i \cdot h_i^2}{\sum_{j=1}^{N} W_j \cdot h_j^2}$$

Why Parabolic ($h_i^2$) instead of Linear ($h_i$)? Per IS 1893:2016 Cl 7.7.1, high-rise buildings deform in a parabolic first-mode flexural profile. Distributing forces with $h_i^2$ correctly concentrates inertial forces at the upper storeys, which is accurately rendered by the amber arrows in Model 1 (Static Loads).

8. Methodological Boundaries & Limitations (Academic Defense)

🚨 Crucial Academic Transparency: Stated Limitations
No structural surrogate is unbounded. During peer review or project viva defense, state these 5 scientific boundaries with confidence:
  1. Plan Regularity Assumption: The archetype models assume regular rectangular footprints ($L/B \le 1.4$) without severe re-entrant corners or C-shapes (IS 1893 Cl 7.1).
  2. Lumped SSI Subgrade Spring: Soil-Structure Interaction is modeled via subgrade compliance and period elongation ($\psi_{\text{SSI}} = 1.05\text{--}1.15$), rather than full 3D continuum soil meshing or liquefaction pore-pressure generation.
  3. Vertical Regularity: Uniform floor heights ($3.5\text{ m}$) are assumed; podium transfer slabs and soft-storey floating columns are excluded.
  4. 1D Hydrodynamic Flood Drag: Yamuna flood loads are modeled via hydrostatic and 1D velocity drag; turbulent 3D debris impact and local scour depth reduction around individual pile caps are omitted.
  5. Monotonic/Spectral Proxy: Non-linear response is calibrated against peak spectral envelopes; high-cycle hysteretic pinching degradation under 50+ reverse-cyclic cycles requires full time-history FEA.
