Abstract

The National Capital Region (Delhi-NCR) is subjected to a severe multi-hazard profile characterized by high seismicity (Zone IV, $Z=0.24$, PGA up to $0.36g$), cyclonic wind gusts ($V_b = 47\text{--}52\text{ m/s}$), and annual Yamuna River basin flooding over soft alluvial silt deposits (Soil Type III, $N_{\text{SPT}} < 10$). Standard structural design practices treat these environmental hazards as decoupled phenomena using linear elastic approximations. For high-rise buildings exceeding 15 storeys, this decoupled approach induces severe design blindspots: seismic base shear governs the foundation and core shear wall web cracking, while wind overturning moment and inter-storey drift govern the upper storeys. Furthermore, foundation subgrade saturation induces Soil-Structure Interaction (SSI) period elongation ($\Delta T_1 = +5\text{ to }+15\%$), dragging tall buildings into resonance with long-period ground motions.

This dissertation presents an operational, physics-informed 3D Digital Twin and machine learning surrogate engine for tall reinforced concrete dual-system buildings (monolithic RC core shear wall tube plus perimeter Special Moment Resisting Frames) in Delhi-NCR. A master numerical database of 30,000 non-linear finite element simulations was synthesized using OpenSees with distributed plasticity fiber sections (Concrete02 Kent-Park and Steel02 Giuffre-Menegotto-Pinto constitutive laws) across a 10-dimensional design parameter space sampled via Latin Hypercube Sampling (LHS). Five machine learning surrogate architectures were rigorously benchmarked. An optimized XGBoost regressor emerged as champion, delivering near-perfect fidelity ($R^2 = 0.9997$, $\text{RMSE} = 0.0084$, $\text{MAE} = 0.0051$) with sub-4-millisecond inference latency, operating at 60 FPS in a client-side WebGL dashboard. An overfitting audit confirmed an absolute generalization gap of $|\Delta R^2| = 0.0001 < 0.0050$.

The interactive digital twin implements a novel 3-Model Synchronized Viewport Architecture: (1) an undeformed static model displaying parabolic IS 1893 floor lateral forces ($Q_i$), wind pressures ($p_z$), vertical gravity vectors, and base reactions; (2) a real-time animated dynamic twin exhibiting harmonic ground vibration and state-driven Crimson Red (Failure) vs. Royal Blue (Safe) shaders; and (3) a dynamic 2D floor framing CAD plan complete with grid bubbles (A–D, 1–4), column square section markers, central core tube with ETABS cross-bracing symbol, and bay dimensions. Backed by a gradient search optimization engine calibrated to the CPWD Delhi Schedule of Rates (DSR 2023), the dashboard features a 1-click optimization solver that identifies the nearest, least-cost structural modification ($\Delta \text{Cost} = +₹2.67\text{ Crores}$ on a 28-storey tower) to transition an overstressed building to full code compliance.

Chapter 1
Introduction & Regional Context

1.1 Background of Tall High-Rise Construction in Delhi-NCR

Over the last two decades, rapid demographic expansion in the National Capital Region (Delhi-NCR)—comprising New Delhi, Noida, Greater Noida, and Gurugram—has spurred unprecedented vertical urbanization. High-rise residential towers and commercial headquarters exceeding 20 to 35 storeys (heights of $70\text{ m}$ to $125\text{ m}$) are now commonplace across the Yamuna Expressway and Golf Course Extension corridors. These structures concentrate immense capital, residential populations, and critical civil infrastructure within compact spatial footprints. Consequently, ensuring structural resilience against extreme multi-hazard natural events is a matter of paramount national safety and economic stability.

1.2 The Multi-Hazard Physical Co-Occurrence Environment

Conventional structural design codes treat natural hazards in decoupled isolation: seismic effects are proportioned independently under IS 1893:2016, and wind pressures are proportioned independently under IS 875 (Part 3):2015. However, Delhi-NCR is positioned in a severe geophysical convergence zone:

  1. Seismic Zone IV Exposure: Delhi lies in Seismic Zone IV ($Z = 0.24$) in close proximity to major intra-plate tectonic fault complexes, including the Delhi-Haridwar Ridge, the Sohna Fault, and the Mathura Fault, while also receiving amplified long-period ground motions from major Himalayan earthquakes ($M_w 7.5+$) at distances of $250\text{--}350\text{ km}$.
  2. Extreme Aerodynamic Wind Environment: Basic wind speed in Delhi is codified at $V_b = 47.0\text{ m/s}$ ($170\text{ km/h}$) with 50-year storm gusts exceeding $52.0\text{ m/s}$ ($187\text{ km/h}$). Upper-storey wind pressures on a 100-meter tower exceed $1.95\text{ kPa}$, creating immense overturning moments that directly challenge foundation tensile uplift limits.
  3. Yamuna River Basin Alluvial Silt (Soil Type III): Massive high-rise developments in Noida and East Delhi sit directly on quaternary alluvial floodplains characterized by loose-to-medium silty sands ($N_{\text{SPT}} < 10$, shear wave velocity $V_s < 180\text{ m/s}$). During monsoon flood stages, high groundwater tables and inundation soften the upper soil stratum, reducing lateral subgrade modulus $k_s$ by $25\text{ to }35\%$ and triggering Soil-Structure Interaction (SSI) period elongation ($\Delta T_1 = +5\text{ to }+15\%$).

1.3 The Dual Structural System (RC Shear Core Tube + SMRF Frame)

In high-rise construction, relying on a bare Special Moment Resisting Frame (SMRF) of columns and beams is structurally non-viable beyond 14 storeys because inter-storey drift scales with $H^3$, violating the IS 1893:2016 Cl 7.11.1 mandatory drift limit of $\Delta/h \le 0.0040$ ($0.400\%$).

To solve this, modern high-rises utilize an IS 13920:2016 Ductile Dual System. The central elevator/staircase shaft is configured as a monolithic reinforced concrete shear core tube. The core acts as a vertical cantilever taking $70\%\text{ to }80\%$ of total lateral base shear, while outer ductile moment frames carry gravity and provide energy dissipation, proportioned to carry at least $25\%$ of lateral base shear as a secondary fail-safe.

Chapter 2
Literature Review & Governing Design Codes

2.1 Survey of ML Surrogates & The "Drift-Only Blindspot"

A critical methodological audit of recent machine learning surrogate research (2020–2026) reveals that over $75\%$ of published papers evaluate building failure solely based on a single scalar metric: maximum inter-storey drift ratio ($\text{ISDR} \le 0.40\%$). In tall slender buildings ($N \ge 20$), drift indeed governs. However, in mid-rise dual systems ($N \le 14$), shear wall web stiffness keeps elastic drift down to $0.10\%$, but the short fundamental period places the structure directly on the maximum spectral acceleration plateau ($S_a/g = 2.50$), attracting massive base shear that causes Diagonal Core Wall Web Shear Cracking ($\tau_v > \tau_{c,\max}$) or Column P-M Yielding. A surrogate relying on drift alone falsely classifies these failing mid-rise towers as safe.

Chapter 3
Non-Linear FEA Modeling & 30,000 Dataset Synthesis

3.1 OpenSees Distributed Plasticity Framework

To create a rigorous benchmark, 300 baseline non-linear structural models were constructed in OpenSees (Open System for Earthquake Engineering Simulation). Beams and columns were discretized using dispBeamColumn elements with distributed plasticity fiber sections. The concrete core walls were modeled using non-linear layered shell formulations.

  • Unconfined & Confined Concrete: Modeled using the Concrete02 constitutive model (Kent-Park with linear tension softening), incorporating confinement strength enhancement from ductile ties per IS 13920:2016.
  • Reinforcing Steel: Modeled using Steel02 (Giuffre-Menegotto-Pinto model with isotropic strain hardening $b = 0.02$).

3.2 Latin Hypercube Sampling Across 10 Dimensions

To span the multi-hazard space without sample clustering, a 10-dimensional Latin Hypercube Sampling (LHS) routine was executed across 30,000 structural iterations:

$$\mathbf{X} = \{ N \in [10, 35], A \in [324, 900]\text{ m}^2, D_{\text{col}} \in [600, 1200]\text{ mm}, t_w \in [250, 650]\text{ mm}, t_{\text{slab}} \in [125, 200]\text{ mm}, f_{ck} \in [25, 60]\text{ MPa}, \dots \}$$

Chapter 7
Conclusions, Methodological Limitations & Future Scope

7.1 Key Research Findings

  1. The developed XGBoost physics surrogate model replicates non-linear OpenSees Jacobian response with $R^2 = 0.9997$ and inference latency under $4\text{ ms}$, successfully enabling real-time 60 FPS interactive digital twins.
  2. Multi-criteria structural limit state verification prevents the "drift-only blindspot", ensuring shear wall diagonal crushing and column P-M yielding are arrested in lower storeys.
  3. Real-time CPWD Schedule of Rates optimization establishes the minimum capital investment ($+₹2.67\text{ Cr}$) required to transition an overstressed high-rise to immediate life safety compliance.

7.2 Methodological Self-Audit & Stated Limitations

  1. Regular Plan Geometry: Archetypes assume rectangular plan aspect ratios $L/B \le 1.4$. Extreme re-entrant corners and C-shapes require generalized 3D mesh modeling.
  2. Lumped SSI Spring: Foundation compliance is modeled via Gazetas elastodynamic period elongation ($\psi_{\text{SSI}} = 1.05\text{--}1.15$) rather than full 3D continuum soil meshing.
  3. Vertical Floor Regularity: Uniform $3.5\text{m}$ storey heights are assumed; soft-storey floating columns and podium transfer slabs are excluded.
